# FPV Gate Padding: Current Experience

> **Note:** This is not a scientific comparison. It reflects our current experience from building and operating FPV racing gates.

## Current favorite

**Multiple layers of roll-stock PE foam covered with PVC truck-tarpaulin fabric**

This combination currently offers the best balance of:

- low cost
- easy sourcing and cutting
- adjustable thickness by adding or removing layers
- adaptability to different gate shapes
- a durable, repairable outer skin

At present, approximately **30–40 mm of total padding** seems like a sensible target.

## PE foam from a roll

We use a PE foam roll approximately **8 mm thick**. Rolls around **10 mm thick** should work just as well.

### Advantages

- very affordable
- quick to cut into suitable strips
- several layers make it easy to achieve the desired thickness
- considerably cheaper than thick sheets or specialist foams

### Disadvantages and experience

Our PE foam performs extremely poorly under prolonged UV exposure. Unprotected areas became so porous that **a single propeller touch broke the foam into fine plastic confetti**. This creates a correspondingly large amount of plastic waste on the field.

**Recommendation:** Cover PE foam as completely and light-tight as possible. Based on our experience so far, inexpensive PE foam should not be assumed to have adequate UV resistance.

## PVC truck tarpaulin as the outer skin

**PVC truck-tarpaulin fabric** has worked very well for us as the protective cover.

### Advantages

- durable and abrasion-resistant
- waterproof
- protects the foam from UV radiation
- comparatively inexpensive
- offcuts can sometimes be sourced cheaply from tarpaulin manufacturers
- can be heat-welded
- damage can be repaired easily with welded patches

### Disadvantages and experience

- it takes some practice to process cleanly
- too much heat can damage the tarpaulin or the foam underneath
- the cover should not be unnecessarily tight, so the foam can still absorb impacts

A **hot-air gun, a suitable welding nozzle and a silicone pressure roller** work well for welding. This normally makes adhesive unnecessary.

## Hot air and PE foam

PE foam can be carefully softened and shaped with hot air.

This can be useful for:

- clean ends
- compressed transitions
- lightly fixing individual areas in place

The temperature window is narrow. A little too much heat causes the foam to collapse quickly. Always test on offcuts first.

## Thick PE/LDPE sheets

Ready-made sheets approximately 30–40 mm thick are an alternative.

### Advantages

- consistent padding thickness
- little assembly work
- clearly defined construction

### Disadvantages

- considerably more expensive than thin roll stock
- large sheets are expensive or difficult to ship
- less flexible for different gate shapes

For now, multiple layers of PE roll foam therefore seem more attractive to us.

## EVA and cross-linked PE foam

These are technically higher-quality alternatives with good impact absorption and durability.

### Advantages

- good mechanical properties
- in some cases, better ageing and recovery characteristics

### Disadvantages

- considerably more expensive
- so far, no clear benefit for an FPV gate that justifies the additional cost

## PU upholstery foam

### Advantages

- soft
- good damping
- sometimes available at low cost

### Disadvantages

- less attractive for permanent outdoor use
- moisture trapped under the cover can become a problem
- mechanically less robust than PE-based solutions

## Pipe insulation

We already have good long-term experience with pipe insulation.

### Advantages

- some products are highly weather- and age-resistant
- easy to install when the dimensions are suitable
- in our previous products, damaged areas tended to tear out in larger pieces instead of disintegrating into fine plastic confetti

### Disadvantages

- large diameters and thick walls become expensive quickly
- suitable sizes for larger gate structures are difficult to find
- this makes a complete cube less attractive on price

Pipe insulation nevertheless remains interesting for smaller elements or special areas.

## Current recommendation

For a cost-conscious rebuild, we would currently start with this system:

**8–10 mm PE foam roll**

→ multiple layers up to approximately **30–40 mm of padding**

→ completely enclose it in **opaque PVC truck-tarpaulin fabric**

→ heat-weld the tarpaulin

For us, this is currently the best compromise between:

- price
- impact protection
- availability
- adaptability
- repairability

## Key lessons

- **Plan for sufficient thickness:** Approximately 30–40 mm currently seems more useful than very thin padding.
- **Protect PE foam from UV:** Exposed areas can become extremely brittle and produce plastic confetti.
- **The outer skin is critical:** PVC tarpaulin protects the foam and takes the actual abrasion.
- **Design for repairability:** PVC patches can be welded on quickly.
- **Do not automatically buy the most expensive foam:** Specialist materials may perform better, but the benefit may not justify the price for an FPV gate.
- **Test before buying in volume:** PE foams that look similar can differ substantially in hardness and durability.

## Conclusion

Our current favorite is **inexpensive PE roll foam in multiple layers, fully enclosed by heat-welded PVC truck-tarpaulin fabric**.

The construction is affordable, flexible and easy to repair. Based on our experience so far, the decisive weakness of inexpensive PE foam is its **UV sensitivity**. Foam should therefore never be left permanently exposed.
